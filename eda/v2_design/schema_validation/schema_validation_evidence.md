# Schema Validation Evidence Report

**Generated:** 2026-02-19  
**Dataset:** 23,311 records (raw), 998 incidents (prototype extraction)  
**Entities extracted:** 8,680 | **Relations extracted:** 16,591

## Executive Summary

1. **`used_in` Removal:** 2,249/16,591 extraction edges (13.6%) are `USED_IN`, but **100% are EQUIPMENT→LOCATION pairs auto-generated by co-occurrence rules** — not from narrative evidence of actual usage relationships. Narrative patterns appear in 9.9% of records but the extractor cannot distinguish "equipment used at location" from "equipment mentioned near location." **Recommend removal** — rule artifacts, not meaningful semantics.
2. **Incident Type → Property:** "Accident" alone would have degree 9,400 — 1884x the prototype mean degree (5.0). 29.1% null rate. All queries use it as a filter. **Strongly recommend property.**
3. **Date → Property:** REPORTED_DATE covers 100.0% of records. At day granularity, 3,127 unique nodes with median degree 6. At year granularity, mega-hub problem recurs. **Strongly recommend property.**
4. **CASE_CATEGORIZATION → Entity:** 117 unique values, 91.9% coverage. 94.6% of incident pairs sharing a category have NO other entity overlap — these are genuinely novel cross-incident connections. **Recommend entity (Root Cause Category).**
5. **Split `caused_by`:** The current L1 pipeline produces 1,237 CAUSED_BY edges — **none** from CASE_CATEGORIZATION (it's not wired as a source). These edges are entity-to-entity pairs with quality issues (e.g., Equipment→"NO ONE WAS INJURED"). Meanwhile, CASE_CATEGORIZATION exists for 91.9% of records but has only 29.6% word overlap with narratives, confirming it's an analyst-assigned label. **Split into `categorized_as` (L1 metadata) + `caused_by` (L2 NLP) is strongly justified** — they are fundamentally different signals that should not share a relation type.
6. **Entity-type relation assignment:** 130 entity spans have multiple type assignments. 44.5% of prototype incidents have both Equipment and Body Part. INVOLVED tail types: {'EQUIPMENT': 2972, 'ORGANIZATION': 2748, 'INCIDENT_TYPE': 1519}; AFFECTED tail types: {'BODY_PART': 1714}. **Entity type alone is sufficient for relation assignment in the vast majority of cases.**
7. **Location hierarchy:** 98.1% of WORKPLACE strings parse into 4-5 components. 2 cities appear under multiple countries (inconsistencies). 710 additional `located_in` edges. **Feasible with minor cleanup.**
8. **Dual-source `resulted_in`:** Narrative finds injuries that IMPACT_TYPE misses in 2056 records. IMPACT_TYPE reports injury without narrative evidence in 7346 cases. **Dual-source with `source` property is justified** — each source catches what the other misses.

**REMOVE `used_in`.** While 13.6% of extraction edges carry this label and 9.9% of narratives contain textual patterns, deeper inspection reveals that **100% of `USED_IN` edges are EQUIPMENT→LOCATION pairs** generated by the deterministic co-occurrence rule `(EQUIPMENT, LOCATION) → USED_IN` in `schema/constants.py:69`. These edges duplicate the incident-level `OCCURRED_AT` signal without adding reliable information. Absorbing them into `INVOLVED` or dropping them entirely simplifies the schema with no semantic loss.

## Analysis 5: `caused_by` Source Conflation

### CAUSED_BY Edge Source Breakdown

- **Total CAUSED_BY edges:** 1,237
- **Metadata-sourced** (tail matches a CASE_CATEGORIZATION value): **0** (0.0%)
- **Narrative-sourced** (tail is an extracted entity with causal context): **1,237** (100.0%)

The current pipeline does **not** inject CASE_CATEGORIZATION into the graph at all — all `CAUSED_BY` edges come from entity-to-entity co-occurrence rules with causal keyword detection. This means CASE_CATEGORIZATION's causal signal (available for 91.9% of records) is entirely **unused**.

### CAUSED_BY Quality Issues

The entity-to-entity `CAUSED_BY` edges show semantic problems:

| Entity Type (tail) | Count | Issue |
|------------|------:|-------|
| INJURY_TYPE | 720 | **Inverted causality** — injuries don't cause equipment; Equipment→"NO ONE WAS INJURED" is nonsensical |
| INCIDENT_TYPE | 303 | **Type leak** — "PROCEDURES", "DEMOBILIZATION OPERATIONS" misclassified as INCIDENT_TYPE |
| EQUIPMENT | 118 | Plausible (equipment failure causing incident) |
| ORGANIZATION | 96 | Weak signal (organization mentioned near causal keywords) |

Only 118/1,237 (9.5%) of current `CAUSED_BY` edges have plausible semantics (Equipment→Equipment).

### Causal Language in Narratives

- Narratives containing causal markers ("due to", "caused by", "failure of", etc.): **6,914/23,311** (29.7%)

### CASE_CATEGORIZATION vs. Narrative Content Overlap

- Records where CASE_CATEGORIZATION words appear in narrative: **6,346/21,414** (29.6%)
- Records where CASE_CATEGORIZATION words do NOT appear in narrative: **15,068/21,414** (70.4%)

This low overlap confirms that CASE_CATEGORIZATION is an **analyst-assigned label**, not a narrative-derived finding. Conflating it with narrative-extracted causal relationships under a single `caused_by` relation mixes signal provenance.

### Example Causal Sentences

1. `...t collided into the dhl truck in the parking area causing a small dent on the backside of the truck. no one was injured....`
2. `...tial damages  update 17/3: no damage to equipment due to proper packing and securing with pallet/crate. the pallet was placed close to a...`
3. `...ice, ip right foot get caught between the stairs, resulting in a forward fall....`
4. `...from the pot, landing on the hand holding the pot causing a minor burn. first aid treatment was given and the worker return to work....`
5. `...eas of the deck that had become damaged/ undercut due to procedures not being followed by the welding contractors. in one particular are...`

### Recommendation

**Split into `categorized_as` (Layer 1, metadata) and `caused_by` (Layer 2, narrative).** The current `caused_by` conflates two fundamentally different signal sources. Metadata-derived categorization is deterministic and available for 91.9% of records. Narrative-derived causation requires NLP extraction and is available where causal language exists (29.7%). Separating them enables proper provenance tracking and avoids misleading causal claims from metadata alone.
